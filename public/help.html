FIELD MANUAL / GETTING STARTED

Plan. Travel. Adapt.

Five operations. Fifteen code missions. One shared deadline.

The False Extraction: evidence and deception pilot

In Stories, choose The False Extraction. It follows Munich to Verona through five operations, with a shared 26-hour deadline and £180 starting reserve. Decode three packets per operation, then open Missions → Evidence board. Cross-reference the unlocked source cards, link the evidence supporting your interpretation and verify your deduction before the onward handoff. Earlier verified conclusions remain available on the board.

A correct deduction uses two game minutes; an unsupported conclusion costs three. Optional deduction clues cost £6, then £10, plus one minute each. In operation 3, verify the RED booking and send it through HQ for £8 and six minutes. This adds a connected investigation to the enemy’s queue: existing searches still happen, and later handoffs can expose you again. The clock and pursuit continue while you analyse. All evidence, route rules and clues work offline after downloading the Alpine map.

50 worldwide escapes

Stories contains fifty five-city journeys across Europe, Asia, the Middle East, South America and Africa. Each has its own dossier, five increasingly difficult operations and fifteen different code-breaking methods. The final stop is extraction. Filter by region or search for a city. Earlier stories and their saved escapes remain available through the archive button.

Your briefing gives the shared deadline and starting reserve. Deadlines reflect the route: a river journey can take days. Planning and analysis consume live time; Go advances the clock by the quoted travel, boarding, transfer and border-processing time. Cash, energy, decoded packets and the pursuing enemy carry through all five operations.

Collect the current amber packet, read its evidence, choose among its six available devices and submit the four-digit code. Fifteen different methodologies are used in each story, drawn from twenty-two offline analytical devices. Only decoding reveals the next packet. Return to Operation closes the dashboard. Device use is free; HQ clues cost £4, £8 and £12. Wrong codes cost two game minutes.

Long-distance services require marked terminals. Coaches have boarding cutoffs and departure windows; rail, boats, ferries and charter flights need connected stations, landings or airfields. Nearby towns sometimes permit a direct walk along mapped streets. Tuk-tuks, horses, camels and horse-and-cart rides appear only in selected local districts, not as global shortcuts. Compare the available choices and their full time and cost before Go.

Try The Alpine Ledger for Munich → Innsbruck → Brenner Pass → Bolzano → Verona. The Three River Seals follows the Amazon; The Strait Contact includes a ferry across the Strait of Gibraltar. All services, contacts, fares and border rules are game fiction. Rail, river and some street geometry is generalized; shore and terminal access may be approximate.

Each route’s map downloads once when you first start it. The game, its rules, puzzles and that saved map then work offline. A route you have not downloaded needs a connection first. Safe addresses can wait out a reported search; optional money and food contacts take travel and collection time.

Your first escape

Open Missions beneath the timer and supplies to see the current packet and destination and the destination. Choose your own stops and transport; Go appears after transport selection. Collection confirms the item and leaves your next move to you.

  1. Choose a story and read the dossier.

    Stories shows 50 escapes across five world regions. Each illustrated briefing describes the plot, five operations and their three sequential code packets. The clock starts when you launch, not while browsing a new story.

  2. Find the first amber mission stop.

    Your red marker is your actual position. Amber numbered badges identify the current mission; decoding it reveals the next. A tick means decoded. £ and ♡ mark optional cash and energy. Open Stop symbols beside the transport legend for the full key. Click a named marker, pick a destination from the list, or select your own point on the map. Required missions are numbered: collect and decode missions 1, 2 and 3 in order.

  3. Choose your transport.

    The transport chooser lists connected options, their fare, duration and estimated energy on arrival. Select one to add that leg. Repeat to plan ahead. Click a planned stop again to remove that stop and every following leg.

  4. Press Go.

    Go travels the planned route; travel is animated while the clock simulates the full journey time. A fresh check runs before every leg. Impossible legs do not charge a fare. Previous completed legs still cost time and money. “Stop after this leg” keeps the remaining plan.

  5. Collect the item when you arrive.

    The route stops at an incomplete mission and opens its pickup card. Press Collect on the card or mission panel. If the next mission is already planned, Go here travels that section and keeps later legs. If you visit mission 2 first, collect mission 1 and return; visiting alone does not complete it. A blocked goal offers an action to collect a missing item or replace the blocked plan with a route to its contact. All three packets must be decoded before the operation destination is allowed. Optional cash and recovery contacts can help but take extra travel and collection time.

  6. Continue the escape.

    Reach the operation destination and press Continue. Cash, energy, inventory and elapsed time carry into the next operation. Each later operation increases journey time, physical effort and mission collection time. The fifth completes the story.

Manage the pressure

Time: one real planning second uses one game second. Travelling, collecting items, rest and enquiries use their quoted game minutes. The displayed route-appropriate countdown spans the entire escape. A border closure countdown, when shown, is a separate earlier limit for crossing.

Energy: walking costs energy. At low energy, a mandatory recovery stop restores energy and adds 25 minutes; it is included in the quote. Other transport can be blocked by insufficient energy. Optional recovery contacts restore up to 45 percentage points. Paid rest costs £6 and 20 minutes.

Money: fares and enquiries draw from the same reserve throughout the escape. Optional cash contacts pay once per operation. The £ symbol represents game credits consistently worldwide, not local currency prices.

Pause: freezes planning and prevents travel, collection and enquiries. Hidden tabs do not use planning seconds. Travel already underway settles its full quoted time and fare. Saves are local to this browser. Opening or reloading always pauses the game, including unfinished travel. Press Resume game to continue. After a regional map upgrade, old untravelled plans are cleared and paused; resources and collected items are preserved.

Optional music and sound effects

Use the ♪ button beside Map view to enable or mute all audio. Sound starts muted. Missions → Help & settings → Settings has separate Music and Sound effects controls and a volume slider. These choices are remembered on this device. The original procedural score and cues work offline without downloads. Music stops when paused, when a dialog is open or when the tab is hidden. Collection and travel cues supplement the visible messages.

Explore beyond the mission region

The OpenFreeMap dark vector basemap loads automatically when online, with sharp worldwide streets and labels. Map view → Dark world map · online switches between it and the offline map; your choice is remembered. It needs internet and loads the area you are viewing, not a global offline download. The offline game road packs and valid mission routes remain unchanged. If the connection drops, the atlas returns to its offline map.

Mission badges keep their number after collection: 1✓ or 2✓ in green. Use Missions → Show on map to find either pickup without planning a route or spending resources.

Offline regional packs

The small core atlas loads first. Opening a world story downloads the two bounded OSM road packs once, then caches them on this device for offline play. The clock is held while they load. Pack sizes vary by city; only the selected corridor is downloaded. These cover the mission districts and airport corridors, not every street in either metropolitan area.

Real-road routing is a game simulation: it uses mapped connectivity, one-way streets and access tags, with short access links where needed. Turn restrictions, signals, traffic and live service availability are not a full navigation model. Do not use it as turn-by-turn travel guidance.

Courier difficulty pilot

Stories → New courier pilot opens The sealed handoff, one standalone operation on the offline Brest map. It is separate from the 50 stories. Collect the sealed documents, then explicitly Deposit documents at Riverside aid point before its counter closes. Deposit removes the documents and adds a receipt; reach Northern pickup with that receipt to finish.

You have 115 minutes, £18 and 60% energy. The counter needs six minutes and must finish by T+75. Shuttles serve only Central, North and Northern pickup, follow the fixed line, cost £4 per boarding and run every 30 minutes with eight-minute boarding. Quotes show the next departure and any document inspection. Walk or use taxis for the unserved contacts. There is no automatic handoff or onward route choice. Ask HQ repeats the service rules and counter window without an extra fee.

Ask HQ

Open Missions → Ask HQ for head-office guidance on your current objective, low resources and known crossing restrictions. The contextual brief has no extra action fee and can be read while paused. Planning time still runs when the game is active. Detailed clues, verification and paperwork use the additional costs shown. In The Shelter Keys, Ask HQ also contains intelligence reports, departures and the document desk.

Stuck?

Open Ask HQ for an operation clue, inspect a cancelled connection, or compare a cheaper mode. Try reaching an airport explicitly before choosing Flight. Check whether both mission items have been collected. You can restart a story from its briefing or practise in the original ten challenges.

Field-desk pilot: The Shelter Keys

Choose The Shelter Keys in Stories to try the five-operation pilot. Open Field desk or Ask HQ for the current report, departures and document desk. Reports show their source, timestamp and age. Dispatch verification costs £2 and five game minutes; an ageing confirmation can be refreshed after 45 minutes. Unverified motor estimates may miss a road delay or suspension. Go checks actual conditions; impossible legs take no fare.

Shuttles and flights use fixed departures. Quotes include waiting and boarding/checks. Missing a cutoff moves you to the next departure; missing the last flight requires restarting the story. Wait 15 game minutes advances time without spending money. All T+ times count from the shared story start; the departure board also shows when this operation began. Report windows refer to that operation start.

Collect both mission items for the document evidence, except operation 3, where the first airport pickup supplies the flight evidence. Select both matching fields and submit. A correct check costs three minutes; a mismatch costs four and grants no clearance. Every operation needs its own clearance; the third also requires it before flying. Reports, clearance and resources are saved offline.

Route colours identify Walk (mint/dotted), Taxi (orange), Shuttle (blue), Cycle (purple) and Flight (gold/dashed). Every planned leg shows an estimated arrival time. These are authored scenario rules.

Back up, restore or restart

Open Missions → Help & settings → Settings → Save backup & recovery. The game pauses while you manage progress. Download backup saves this run as a JSON file; keep it somewhere safe. Choose backup file shows the story, operation, resources and carried items before you confirm. Nothing is uploaded.

Restart this escape starts from its beginning with the original resources. Before a restart or restore, the game keeps one recovery copy on this device. Recover previous run lets you review and restore that copy. The next restart or restore replaces it. Cancel leaves your run unchanged.

Restored games stay paused until Resume. An unfinished journey keeps its quoted fare and time and settles once after arrival. Backups do not contain maps, sound settings or completion records. World regions must already be downloaded for offline play. Clearing site storage removes the local recovery copy; downloaded backup files remain wherever you saved them.

HQ inbox and enemy intelligence

The header HQ inbox is always available. Its amber badge counts unread messages; opening it marks received reports as read. The amber enemy marker and map key show the last confirmed area and report age in game minutes. After 15 minutes, or once the reported search ends, the report is marked STALE: the enemy may have moved. Click the marker to read HQ or the key report to centre that area. Pause freezes report age and the world clock.

All 50 stories, the legacy campaign and The watched route include an enemy pursuer across operations. Watched handoffs, shuttle tickets and flights leave traces. Response times shorten in later operations. ◈ marks trusted shelters, including catalogue contacts and bonus stops. Each shelter is usable once per operation and quotes its full waiting time and energy recovery. HQ offers a free 15-minute wait with no concealment. Hiding and waiting spend the shared deadline. Practice challenges and the standalone courier lab remain training options. Existing saves keep progress and pause when pursuit is added; previous actions create no retroactive traces.

The watched handoff: pursuit and hiding

Open Stories → New pursuit pilot. This separate courier operation gives you 150 minutes, £24 and a deposit deadline of T+95. Collect the documents, deposit them for a receipt, then escape. Hand-offs and named shuttle tickets expose a reported area. Walking and taxis leave no new ticket trace.

HQ warns you with a source, timestamp and expected search window. Amber map areas are approximate reports, not an enemy tracker. The pursuer travels along the offline road network and completes each search before responding to another trace. Leave the area before the warning grace expires or use a hiding place. Searches affect stationary reported areas and boarding waits, not an unseen moving traveller. Known unsafe shuttle boarding is refused without a fare.

◈ marks the workshop back room (sealed documents required), riverside safe house (deposit receipt required), and free station luggage alcove. Click a shelter when you reach it or open Ask HQ. Check its quoted time, fee and energy recovery before pressing Hide. Hiding advances game time until the known local search has passed; it is not the Pause button. Each shelter is usable once and future traces may expose you again. Shelters refuse entry if waiting would miss a mission deadline. Pursuit, shelter use and read HQ reports survive saved games and backup files.

The watched route: five operations

Open Stories → The watched route · Full five-operation story. Start with £180 and 85% energy. The same 24-hour countdown covers all five operations; money, energy, documents, pursuit and used hiding places carry over.

  1. Collect and deposit the watched briefing, then reach shelter.
  2. Recover the passport and submit the manifest before heading to Itami. No required mission sends you back into the city after reaching the airport.
  3. Check the original manifest at Itami, open Ask HQ and match both clearance fields. Fly to Haneda, receive the bag there and continue to reception.
  4. Collect the relay packet, then read HQ’s new delivery address. The amber mission badge moves to the authorised desk. Existing plotted legs stay in your plan: remove or change them yourself.
  5. Collect the extraction token, deposit the ledger and reach the final pickup on foot.

Flight departures are 7, 9, 11 and 13 hours after the story begins: T+420, T+540, T+660 and T+780. The countdown shows time left; T+ shows time used. Boarding and airport checks must finish before departure. Read the quote and clearance desk. Flight manifests become a trace after takeoff; an earlier airport search can still block boarding.

Every operation offers optional cash and energy. Reported police checkpoints add quoted time to motor routes through their map areas while active; walking avoids that inspection delay. New pursuit reports become more urgent in later operations, but older searches keep their original timings. Shelter fees, waiting and entry requirements are shown before use. Pause freezes the world. Endings reflect the evidence delivered, reserves and shelter choices.

On the ending screen, Next story opens the library. Your completed watched-route run remains saved. Backup and recovery work for all five operations, including a flight awaiting Resume.

Three code missions per operation

Each five-operation story has 15 code missions. Only your current mission is marked. Travel to its contact and collect the packet, then open OSINT. Read the source evidence and select an analysis device: Signal decoder, Metadata inspector, Registry search, Time-zone lab, Coordinate matcher or Cipher lab. Enter the evidence into the tool, interpret its output, and submit the four-digit code, including any leading zeros. Correct decoding completes the mission and reveals the next packet.

The workbench does not pause the clock or the enemy. Leave a watched contact before studying if needed. Device use is free; optional HQ clues cost £4, then £8, then £12. The last clue explains the solution. Wrong four-digit submissions cost two game minutes. A successful code is never charged twice. Mission 3 is a separate packet: follow its newly revealed marker and collect it after decoding mission 2. After all three are decoded, choose your route to the operation destination.

All evidence and tools work offline. Photo metadata, registries and signals are fictional puzzle material. Clues and solved packets are saved. Practice challenges, standalone pilots and the legacy campaign keep their earlier rules.
